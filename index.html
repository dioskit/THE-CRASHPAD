Who we are

An outdoor collective rooted in the Indian Himalayas

Crashpad brings together adventure sports, experiential learning, movement practices, festivals and community led initiatives.

Working across Suru Valley in Ladakh and Zoom Rock Garden in Sikkim, we create opportunities for people to learn new skills, take part in outdoor activities, experience different movement practices and connect through shared experiences.

From school programmes and climbing sessions to festivals, courses, mountain biking and music gatherings, our work brings people together through the outdoors.

Experiential learning

What we believe about learning

“Some of the most meaningful learning happens beyond the classroom.”

Experiential learning is learning by doing, exploring and reflecting on real world experiences. Whether it's climbing a rock face, building with natural materials, navigating a trail or working together to solve a challenge, every experience is a chance to develop new skills, build confidence, strengthen relationships and discover new perspectives.

At Crashpad, learning isn't just about acquiring knowledge. It's about putting it into practice, learning from the experience, and carrying those lessons into everyday life.

How we teach

  1. 01Do

    Start with a real experience: a climb, a build, a trail, a team challenge.

  2. 02Reflect

    Pause together: what happened, what felt hard, what worked.

  3. 03Make sense

    Connect it to skills and ideas: problem solving, collaboration, the natural world.

  4. 04Apply

    Try again, take on more, and carry the lesson into everyday life.

Hands, earth & time

Natural Building

We build with what the land provides (earth, stone, bamboo, lime and timber) using traditional and ecological methods to create beautiful, functional, low impact structures.

Natural building is both a craft and a philosophy. Every project is site specific: we work with local materials to reduce embodied energy, and we build with many hands, so the people who use a space are part of making it.

  • Local firstSoil, stone and bamboo from the site or close by, so very little is trucked in.
  • Built for the climateThick earthen walls hold warmth on cold nights and stay cool through the day.
  • Breathable & non toxicClay and lime let walls breathe, without cement or synthetic finishes.
  • Returns to the landMaterials that can be repaired, reshaped, or go back into the ground.
  • Learning by doingEvery build doubles as a workshop for volunteers, students and the local community.

Techniques we use

Earthbag

Bags packed with moist subsoil, laid in courses and tamped solid. Strong, fast and ideal for foundations and round walls.

Cob & Rammed Earth

Sculpted or compacted earthen walls that breathe, regulate temperature and last generations.

Bamboo & Timber Framing

Structural joinery for roofs, shelters and pavilions, shaped and fitted by hand.

Stone Masonry

Dry stone and mortared work with local rock for plinths, paths and retaining walls.

Lime & Clay Plasters

Breathable finishes that protect earthen walls and give them their warm, handmade surface.

From ground to roof

  1. 01Read the site

    Test the soil, find the stone, follow the sun and water.

  2. 02Prepare the earth

    Sieve, mix and wet the soil to the right consistency.

  3. 03Raise the walls

    Course by course, packed and tamped by many hands.

  4. 04Frame & roof

    Timber and bamboo joinery, then thatch or sheet.

  5. 05Finish & gather

    Plaster, light it up, and bring people in.
